# Part 1: Day one

## Log in

Open your brain's address in a browser.
The browser asks for the console password.
Whoever set up your brain gave you both; keep the password in a password manager.
The conversation is the home screen; "everything" at the top right lists every other page.

## Give your brain a reasoner

On the home screen, paste your own API key into the card, or press "Sign in with a Claude subscription" and follow the door.
Your key or your sign-in stays on your server.
Nobody else's account is ever used for you, and you should never use someone else's.

## Name it, then talk to it

A new brain has no name yet.
Press "Name your brain" and fill in its name and yours; that writes its identity, its persona.
Then ask it something real, like "what am I working on?".
A specific answer means the brain is working; a generic one means it needs more about you (below).

If you want the console to look your way, say "personalize the console".
It asks ten short questions and ends with a card; press apply.
You can change any answer later on the Layout page.

## First documents

Memory comes from documents you give it on the Knowledge tab.
Before you upload, pick the Privacy chip: open (the default) or local-only (see [Part 4](04-memory-and-privacy.md)).
Notes about yourself, your work and how you think help most.

A file you attach in the conversation (the attach button, or drop it on the box where you type) is different.
It is saved on your server for the reasoner to read in that conversation; it does not become memory unless you also upload it on Knowledge.

## The phone door

The console works in a phone browser, with the same address and password.
Your brain can also have a Telegram bot, if the owner of the server sets one up.
The first chat that writes to it becomes its owner; everyone else is told the brain is private.
Cards arrive on the phone as "May I?" with Allow and Refuse.
Voice notes are transcribed; photos and files land on your server.
Everything you send through Telegram passes through Telegram's servers.

## The seven-step tutorial

Open Guide, then Tutorial.
Seven things to do once; each turns done by itself when the brain has seen it happen.
